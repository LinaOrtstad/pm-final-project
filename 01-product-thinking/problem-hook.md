## Responses
- **Chosen path, which Riverty business-unit case are you using?:** BU P&C
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** Riverty would not collapse in 12 months. The more likely outcome is that its position gets weaker in a way that doesn’t show up in its numbers until it is hard to reverse. The erosion would come from the consumer side, which is the part a merchant-first strategy depends on but doesn’t own.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** Searching inbox for Riverty emails, opening banking app for checking balances, setting calneder reminders on when to pay, Using a bank card, Apple Pay or a rival’s card, because Riverty has no card in the wallet
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** Riverty’s shoppers already juggle bills, due dates and rival apps to manage what they owe, and while competitors like Klarna turn that daily need into card and app habits (5 million active card users and counting), Riverty stays an invisible checkout button, so the consumer relationship, and eventually the merchants that follow it, are slipping away to someone else.
- **Value proposition, based on the initiative in your assigned Riverty case, what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** For repeat Riverty shoppers, the Riverty card is the everyday way to pay that shows what you owe and when, and lets you choose to pay now, split or pay later, without fee surprises. Unlike a bank card or a rival’s app, it is built on the same trusted checkout relationship you already have with the merchants you shop at.

Launch now, starting with a wallet-ready virtual card and due-date nudges, because every quarter without a daily presence lets competitors own the consumer relationship that Riverty’s merchants depend on.
