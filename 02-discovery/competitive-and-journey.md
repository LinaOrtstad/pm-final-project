# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A regular pay later user who pays in shops by tapping a bank card on autopilot and would use a pay later card daily if it worked at places like the supermarketv
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Use one card everywhere, including splitting the big weekly shop, without thinking about it.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** The card is declined at a bakery, and the app gives no reason (N-06, T-101). The participant went back to their debit card the same week. The card never gets a second chance, and the participant files no ticket, so the loss is invisible. For many users the moment comes even earlier: 143 activated cards never reached the phone wallet (T-102), so tap-to-pay was never available to them.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Default to the regular bank card
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** The workaround, step by step
Default to the bank card (documented). In shops this persona taps their bank card “without thinking” (N-03). This is the existing tool, and the Riverty card has to displace it. Nothing in the pilot gives them a reason to change the habit.
Attempt the Riverty card, or never get that far (documented and inferred). For the 143 activated cards that never reached the phone wallet (T-102), the attempt may never happen. The pack doesn’t say what these users do, but since online purchases don’t need the wallet, the likely result is a card that only works online.
Hit a decline with no explanation (documented). At the bakery the app gave no reason (N-06, T-101).
Switch to the debit card at the till (documented). The participant returned to the debit card the same week.
Pay twice when the notification is late (documented, for some users). When a push arrives minutes late, participants assume the payment failed and pay again with another card (T-108).
Keep using Riverty only where it already works (inferred). The persona’s pay later behavior stays online (N-01) or in their other apps (N-02). For the weekly shop they want to split (N-04), no workaround is documented, and the likeliest default is paying in full with the bank card.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** It is a manual fallback with no feedback loop. The user decides on the spot, with a queue behind them, whether to try again, try another card, or give up. With no decline reason, they can’t tell whether the problem was a limit, a rule or a terminal fault, so retrying is a gamble.
It risks financial side effects. Paying again after a late notification (T-108) can mean a double charge and a refund to chase, which pushes cost back onto the user and the support team.
It defeats the persona’s goal. They wanted “one card everywhere” without thinking. The fallback means carrying and remembering two cards, which is the mental overhead N-02 and N-03 describe.
It leaves their main need unmet. Splitting the big weekly shop (N-04) is a high-value use for pay later. If the card fails at the till, that need goes to the bank card or goes unmet.
It is permanent after one failure. N-06 went back to debit “the same week.” One bad experience set the habit, and nothing in the pack suggests a path back.
