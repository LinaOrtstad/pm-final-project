Slide 5 · Strategy

Riverty stays an invisible checkout button

Problem hook · BU P&C

Riverty’s shoppers already juggle bills, due dates and rival apps to manage what they owe, and while competitors like Klarna turn that daily need into card and app habits (5 million active card users and counting), Riverty stays an invisible checkout button, so the consumer relationship, and eventually the merchants that follow it, are slipping away to someone else.

Value proposition

For repeat Riverty shoppers, the Riverty card is the everyday way to pay that shows what you owe and when, and lets you choose to pay now, split or pay later, without fee surprises. Unlike a bank card or a rival’s app, it is built on the same trusted checkout relationship you already have with the merchants you shop at.

Launch now, starting with a wallet-ready virtual card and due-date nudges, because every quarter without a daily presence lets competitors own the consumer relationship that Riverty’s merchants depend on.

Moment of misery

Searching inbox for Riverty emails, opening banking app for checking balances, setting calneder reminders on when to pay, Using a bank card, Apple Pay or a rival’s card, because Riverty has no card in the wallet

Cold-read check

High business risk if not up to date with market and competitors · a systematic problem · the value proposition actually removes the obstacle.

Data-backed hypothesis

Based on a regular pay later user who pays in shops by tapping a bank card on autopilot and would use a pay later card daily if it worked at places like the supermarket, in-store share of purchases today never exceeded 17.7%. I believe that the ability to use one card everywhere, including splitting the big weekly shop, without thinking about it, will result in the user using the Riverty card for all their purchases, as measured by a 50% increase in in-store share of purchases. I will protect in-store decline rates (remain the same) and will make a go/no-go decision after 6 months.

Slide 6 · Research

Competitive analysis and workaround

Role
A regular pay later user who pays in shops by tapping a bank card on autopilot and would use a pay later card daily if it worked at places like the supermarket
Goal
Use one card everywhere, including splitting the big weekly shop, without thinking about it.
External tool
Default to the regular bank card
Friction
The card is declined at a bakery, and the app gives no reason (N-06, T-101). The participant went back to their debit card the same week. For many users the moment comes even earlier: 143 activated cards never reached the phone wallet (T-102).
The workaround, step by step
  • Default to the bank card (documented). Taps it “without thinking” (N-03).
  • Attempt the Riverty card, or never get that far (documented and inferred).
  • Hit a decline with no explanation (documented).
  • Switch to the debit card at the till (documented).
  • Pay twice when the notification is late (documented, for some users; T-108).
  • Keep using Riverty only where it already works (inferred).
Core frustration · the evidence

It is a manual fallback with no feedback loop. With no decline reason, they can’t tell whether the problem was a limit, a rule or a terminal fault, so retrying is a gamble. They wanted “one card everywhere” without thinking. The fallback means carrying and remembering two cards, which is the mental overhead N-02 and N-03 describe.

PRD highlights · MUST HAVE for B1
Visible in seconds.
Every in-store decline shows up in the app within seconds, with merchant, amount and time.
Plain-language reason.
A short fixed set, plus a fallback for unmapped codes. No silent declines.
Retry-or-switch signal.
One line: “try again” or “pay another way now”.
Push to the declined transaction.
A push notification that opens straight to it.
Constraint

No change to approval logic, and no exposure of risk or fraud rules in the copy. This protects the decline-rate guardrail, and the feature explains declines without changing them.

Slide 8 · Validation

Experiment plan: Decline reason and retry hint

Hypothesis

I believe that Decline reason and retry hint for a regular pay later user who pays in shops by tapping a bank card on autopilot will result in the user being able to use one card everywhere, including splitting the big weekly shop, without thinking about it, as measured by a +2 pts change in in-store share of purchases within 1 month.

Control (A) · today

The card is declined at a bakery, and the app gives no reason (N-06, T-101). The participant went back to their debit card the same week. 143 activated cards never reached the phone wallet (T-102).

Variant (B) · single change

Notification and activity feed entry point, a decline detail screen with reason (12 words or fewer) and one hint (“Try again” or “Pay another way”), then a resolution screen. Deliberately absent: contact links, a detailed view, any risk wording.

Primary metric

Ship

Iterate

Direction is positive but lift is below MDE.

Kill

The primary metric shows no improvement or moves negatively.

Isolation check, identical between arms: app version, eligibility rules, onboarding to app. Read date fixed at the end of 1 month; no results reviewed before then.
